01 · WHY

Why this needs to run offline

India is home to an estimated 18 million deaf people — the WHO puts the number of Indians with significant hearing loss as high as 63 million. Yet the country has roughly 300 certified Indian Sign Language interpreters, concentrated in a handful of cities.

For nearly everyone else, day-to-day communication with hospitals, banks, police stations, classrooms and shops depends on a family member translating, or doesn't happen at all — and none of that works if the "solution" is a cloud app that needs a data plan or a subscription. A bridge like this only matters if it works in a village clinic with no signal, at 2am, for free, forever. That's a Hexagon NPU problem, not a cloud API problem.

02 · UNDER THE HOOD

How the demo in front of you actually works

Nothing here is simulated. Your camera feed is read locally, hand landmarks are computed by a model running in your browser's WebAssembly runtime, and the gesture classifier is plain geometry on those 21 keypoints — no frame is ever sent anywhere. Only the numbers 1–5 in this demo are genuine ISL signs (ISL numerals follow the same finger-count convention); the fist, thumbs-up, 👌 and 🤟 shapes are common international gestures included to show the pipeline working, not claimed as ISL — see the honesty note below.

01

Capture

Webcam frame grabbed locally via getUserMedia. Never leaves the device.

02

Landmark detection

MediaPipe Hand Landmarker locates 21 keypoints per hand, on-device, in WASM.

03

Geometric classification

Finger-extension angles and distances are mapped to a starter vocabulary of 9 signs.

04

Stabilize

A sign must hold for ~8 consecutive frames before it's committed, to reject motion blur.

05

Voice out

Committed sign is captioned and spoken with the on-device speech synthesizer.

04 · ROADMAP

Honest scope, and what's next

This starter vocabulary is static, single-hand, and closer to international finger-counting and common gestures than to grammatically complete Indian Sign Language, which is two-handed, continuous, and uses facial grammar. Overclaiming that would be dishonest to the deaf community this is meant to serve — so here's the real path from here.

  • Phase 1 — now

    Static single-hand vocabulary

    9 hand shapes running in the browser today: numbers 1–5 (genuine ISL numerals) plus four labeled generic gestures (fist, thumbs-up, 👌, 🤟) that demonstrate the pipeline beyond counting, rule-based, no training required.

  • Phase 2

    True ISL word signs

    Fine-tune a lightweight classifier on real ISL hand-shapes using open datasets, replacing hand geometry rules with a learned model compiled for QNN.

  • Phase 3

    Two-handed, continuous signing

    Track both hands plus upper-body pose, add temporal modeling for sentence-level ISL using resources like the ISLTranslate corpus.

  • Phase 4

    Bidirectional and multilingual

    On-device Whisper for speech-in, on-device TTS in Hindi and regional languages for speech-out — a full two-way conversation bridge on a single Snapdragon PC.
